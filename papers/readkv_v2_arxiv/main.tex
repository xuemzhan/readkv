\begin{abstract}
Cross-model key--value (KV) cache handoff is evaluated on paired counterfactual
benchmarks. We audit one such benchmark for Qwen3-1.7B$\to$0.6B and find 18
degenerate must-flip pairs, at least 15 groups of verbatim cross-split document
overlap, and shared template skeletons between calibration and held-out data.
Rebuilding the corpus reverses the apparent success of an affine mapper: its
native-minus-transferred dual-correctness gap is $-5.0$ to $3.3$ percentage
points in the original/original condition, versus $23.3$ to $61.7$ points in
the other nine cell--seed combinations of a 2$\times$2 corpus-version design.
On the rebuilt validation sets the transferred counterfactual error is no
larger than mismatched, zero and randomized cache controls, and is
significantly smaller in seed~0 (family-level paired $p=0.010$ against the
zero-cache control), even though dual correctness collapses to
$28.3$--$41.7\%$. A pre-declared equivalence test with margin $\delta=2$ log-
probability units succeeds in seed~1 for all three controls and in seed~2 for
the randomized control only, so ``indistinguishable from a zero-information
control'' is not uniformly supported. We further show that counterfactual
error has weak discriminative validity: its family intervals overlap between
the affine mapper and a distillation baseline whose dual correctness differs
by $28$--$45$ points, and its rank correlation with correctness reverses sign
between the two ($-0.436$ versus $+0.435$). Scoring complete candidate
sequences preserves the direction of the version contrast in every seed but
lowers its magnitude by $21.7$ points in one seed ($61.7\to40.0$ points on all
60 validation pairs). All estimates are exploratory (at most eight template
families), cover one synthetic model pair, and do not attribute the reversal
to any single defect. Single-defect interventions, a sealed test, a second
model pair and a published external baseline remain unevaluated. We release
the repaired benchmark, the numeric ledger and the audit protocol.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Cache translation lets a receiver answer a query from a document processed by
a different model, without prefilling that document again (\emph{strict zero
re-prefill}). Downstream answer correctness indicates whether the transmitted
cache is useful; a paired-document design can additionally ask whether the
receiver's candidate scores change when a fact in the document changes. These
two quantities can diverge, and diagnosing a divergence presupposes a valid
document pair and a receiver that can answer under both variants
(Section~\ref{sec:metrics}).

An earlier analysis of a synthetic paired benchmark suggested that an affine
KV mapper preserved dual-side correctness while changing counterfactual
response scores, and suggested a receiver-side value-consumption mechanism. We
withdrew both claims after auditing the benchmark: the legacy corpus contained
degenerate pairs, cross-split document overlap and overlapping template
skeletons, and its uncertainty estimates effectively treated related examples
as independent (Section~\ref{sec:dataaudit}). The general reproducibility
concern is discussed by \citet{kapoor2023}; this paper is a concrete audit of
one benchmark rather than a general lesson about leakage.

\paragraph{What this study establishes.}
We cross the original and rebuilt calibration and validation corpora for one
model pair and three data seeds, giving twelve cell--seed combinations
(Section~\ref{sec:audit2x2}). Replacing either corpus moves the native-minus-
transferred correctness gap from near zero to $23.3$--$61.7$ percentage
points. We then ask two questions that the version contrast alone cannot
answer. First, how does the transferred counterfactual error compare with
explicit no-information controls once per-seed cluster intervals and a
pre-declared equivalence margin are used (Section~\ref{sec:controltest})?
Second, does counterfactual error actually track whether transfer is useful
(Section~\ref{sec:metricvalid})? Both are answered from archived scores
without new model runs. A third check asks whether the direction survives
scoring complete candidate sequences rather than only their first token
(Section~\ref{sec:seqscore}).

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(1)~A defect taxonomy and audit protocol covering degenerate pairs,
cross-split leakage, entity and template-skeleton overlap, and clustered
bootstrap degeneracy. (2)~A repaired paired benchmark (\texttt{cf\_pairs\_v2.1})
with a released numeric ledger and a CPU replay. (3)~Controlled evidence that
the correctness gap is sensitive to corpus version, together with control,
equivalence and metric-validity diagnostics: counterfactual error does not
separate a useful from a useless mapper, whereas dual correctness does.
(4)~A scoring-readout check showing that direction survives but magnitude does
not.

\paragraph{Scope of claims.}
This is an audit of one equal-depth model pair on synthetic Chinese template
data, with at most eight template families and a sealed test that remains
unevaluated. We do not attribute the reversal to any single defect, do not
claim a mechanism on clean data, do not claim cross-model or cross-pair
generalization, and do not claim that the equivalence tests are well powered.
A failed equivalence test is reported as inconclusive rather than as evidence
of a deficit. The estimates are exploratory throughout. Single-defect
interventions, a sealed test, a second model pair and a published external
baseline remain unevaluated; these are stated once here and not repeated in
the body.

\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Related work.}
\citep{heo2026} describe closed-form KV mapping and report substantial
degradation on some model pairs. \citep{kvlingo} use distillation to train KV
translators, and \citep{cachelink} study differences between reconstruction
fidelity and receiver utility. A cross-version study names \emph{Functional
Cache Divergence}~\citeCacheDivergence; a fidelity--consumability study reports
that substitute-cache methods can remain at the no-information baseline and
that ``more faithful'' need not mean ``consumable''~\citeFidelityConsumability;
a safety study finds value-reuse bias exceeding key-reuse bias and applies
per-layer/per-head affine correction~\citeValueReuse. Strong transfer baselines
and failure analyses---sparse query-relevant transfer~\citeSparseTransfer,
prefill-free cross-family transfer~\citePrefillFree, rank-disagreement
selective recomputation~\citeRankRecompute, and training-free cache
fusion~\citeCacheFusion---and hybrid/linear attention
translation~\citeHybridAttention further delimit the method space. A causal
audit of relayed KV caches uses mismatched, zero and randomized cache controls
with an equivalence test~\citeCacheAuditControls; our control-group design
uses the same idea. Paired minimally changed inputs follow contrast-set
evaluation~\citep{contrastsets} and counterfactually augmented
data~\citep{kaushik2020}; answer accuracy can be preserved while evidence
faithfulness degrades under cache compression~\citep{acc_evidence}. The audit
addresses leakage and dependent evaluation units~\citep{kapoor2023}. Our
contribution is the calibration-by-validation comparison together with its
control, metric and scoring diagnostics. Related-work characterizations in
this arXiv version were checked at abstract level only; full-text verification
was not completed. The earlier SQuAD anchor~\citep{squad16,squad18} was
floor-limited and is not used as evidence here (Section~\ref{sec:anchor}).

\section{Research Questions}
\label{sec:rq}

The study is organized around four questions.

\begin{description}
\item[RQ1 (corpus version).] Does replacing the calibration or validation
      corpus change the native-minus-transferred dual-correctness gap?
      Evidence: the defect inventory and repair report
      (Section~\ref{sec:datafix}), the before/after version contrast
      (Section~\ref{sec:reversal}), and the controlled 2$\times$2 contrast
      (Section~\ref{sec:audit2x2}).
\item[RQ2 (no-information controls).] How does the transferred counterfactual
      error compare with mismatched, zero and randomized caches once
      per-seed family intervals, paired tests and a pre-declared equivalence
      margin are used? Evidence: Section~\ref{sec:controltest}.
\item[RQ3 (metric validity).] Does counterfactual error rank mappers by
      dual-side correctness, and is its scale explained by the Self reference?
      Evidence: Sections~\ref{sec:metricvalid} and~\ref{sec:kdbase}.
\item[RQ4 (scoring readout).] Does the direction of the version contrast
      survive scoring complete candidate sequences, and how does its magnitude
      change? Evidence: Section~\ref{sec:seqscore}.
\end{description}

\section{Data and Audit}
\label{sec:dataaudit}

\subsection{Counterfactual data construction: slices F1--F6 and denominators}
\label{sec:data}

Each evaluation unit is $u=(q,d_A,d_B,a,b,m)$: a query and a pair of documents
differing in one targeted fact, with candidates A/B held fixed within the pair
and their order randomly swapped. Six slices exclude distinct confounds:
F1 direct fact substitution; F2 reasoning-chain change; F3 relation/negation
reversal; F4 distractors and coreference; F5 surface equivalence (a
\emph{non-flip negative control}); F6 length/position control. The rebuilt
corpus has 520 pairs (probe 40 / train 120 / val 60 / test 300 / \texttt{real 0}).

\paragraph{Three distinct denominators.} The number $398$ is the
\emph{corpus-level} count of must-flip pairs after excluding F5 and F6; it is
not the denominator of any per-run metric. Each validation cell contains 60
pairs, of which 46 belong to F1--F4 (the only slices with a must-flip gold
label used here). The per-pair flip metric is further restricted to the
Self-valid subset $|D_{\mathrm{self}}|\ge0.1$. For the reconstructed affine
cells this yields, after the F1--F4 and Self-threshold filters, $34/45$,
$35/46$, and $34/46$ evaluable must-flip pairs for seeds 0/1/2 (frozen
per-pair logs). We report these slice-level denominators alongside the
all-slice algorithm that produced the frozen summary tables, and treat the
latter \texttt{exploratory}.

\paragraph{First-token proxy and F5/F6 behaviour.} The score $g$ in
Section~\ref{sec:metrics} is a \emph{binary proxy}: it uses the log-probability
of the candidate \emph{first token} at one shared generation position, not a
full-sequence likelihood. Under this readout, F5 pairs have identical candidate
texts, so the proxy error is exactly zero and dual-side correctness is $1$ for
F5; F5 therefore has no discriminative power for this metric and is excluded
from must-flip denominators. F6 changes the person name \emph{and} the gold
label (the answer still swaps between A and B); it is a length/position control
and is likewise excluded from the frozen flip denominator. Full candidate-text
(candidate-sequence) scoring is evaluated as a robustness check pre-declared
in an internal version-controlled protocol, not an external registration, in
Section~\ref{sec:seqscore}.

contradiction.

\paragraph{Real documents.} Real-document collection was blocked by
licence/network, semantic-suitability and registration constraints: no real
split, manifest entry or run exists (\texttt{real=0}), so real-document
external validity remains unverified.

\paragraph{AI-generated data.} A proposal to generate HellaSwag-seeded AI
counterfactual data was evaluated but not activated; no generated dataset or
result exists.

\paragraph{Residual boundaries.} All data are synthetic templates from a
finite entity pool; even with 24 base structures they do not represent
real-world distributions. The \texttt{real=0} condition is described above;
all rebuilt-corpus evidence comes from the equal-depth MVP pair
(1.7B$\to$0.6B). The 61 length-mismatch pairs fall in F5 (non-flip control)
and are reported as a separate stratum. Clean-data intervals use eight
template families and are exploratory; the sealed test was not evaluated.

\section{Method and Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Setup, notation, and arm boundary}
\label{sec:proposed}

A teacher $T$ prefill-computes a cache $C_T(d)$ of document $d$; a mapper
$g_\theta$ maps it into receiver $S$'s cache format
$\hat{C}_S(d)=g_\theta(C_T(d))$. The receiver decodes query $q$ from
$\hat{C}_S(d)$ and never re-processes $d$. The receiver's own cache $C_S(d)$ is
an \emph{offline Oracle}: it may be used for training targets, evaluation
anchors, and intervention, but never inside the deployable inference path.
$S$'s main weights are frozen. The two documents of a counterfactual pair,
$d_A$ and $d_B$, are prefilled \emph{independently}; cache bytes must never be
tampered with to synthesize the other document.

In the clean-data results the mapping arm is a closed-form ridge affine map
with \emph{zero gradient-optimization epochs}: it estimates affine coefficients
from calibration data but has no gradient-trained parameters (roughly $7.4$M
matrix and intercept coefficients). We distinguish four
arms by the provenance of Key and Value (Table~\ref{tab:arms}). Only \emph{Joint}
is deployable under strict zero re-prefill: the student processes no document
token and does not read $C_S(d)$ at inference. A forward hook asserts that no
document token enters the student and writes a per-run
\texttt{zero\_re\_prefill} flag. K-only, V-only, Self, Identity, and Oracle are
offline diagnostics or anchors and are not deployable methods.

\subsection{Metrics: $E_{\mathrm{CF}}$ and joint reporting}
\label{sec:metrics}

For a candidate pair, define $g(C,q)=\log P(A\mid C,q)-\log P(B\mid C,q)$ at a
single shared generation position, where $P(A\mid C,q)$ and $P(B\mid C,q)$ are
the probabilities of the \emph{candidate first tokens} A and B
(Section~\ref{sec:data}). Then
\begin{align}
D_{\mathrm{self}} &= g(C_S(d_A),q)-g(C_S(d_B),q),\\
D_{\mathrm{trans}} &= g(\hat C_S(d_A),q)-g(\hat C_S(d_B),q),\\
E_{\mathrm{CF}} &= |D_{\mathrm{trans}}-D_{\mathrm{self}}|,\qquad
\mathrm{normalized}\ E_{\mathrm{CF}}=\frac{|D_{\mathrm{trans}}-D_{\mathrm{self}}|}
{|D_{\mathrm{self}}|+\tau},\qquad \tau=1.0 .
\end{align}
$E_{\mathrm{CF}}$ is a non-negative absolute difference at the first-token
level and equals zero for Self and Identity \emph{by definition}; the
template-family interval excluding zero describes error magnitude but cannot
by itself establish a practically meaningful utility--fidelity separation.
$E_{\mathrm{CF}}$ is not a quality measure by itself: when
$D_{\mathrm{self}}\approx0$, a constant output can achieve small
$E_{\mathrm{CF}}$. It is therefore always jointly reported with dual-side
per-document success \texttt{dual\_correct}, direction consistency
\texttt{flip\_consistency} on the Self-valid subset $|D_{\mathrm{self}}|\ge0.1$,
coverage \texttt{coverage}, and the gap to no-information controls
\texttt{info\_attribution} (control-group $E_{\mathrm{CF}}$ minus Joint
$E_{\mathrm{CF}}$). Because the control groups are separate arms, the per-pair
\texttt{info\_gap} field is unset and the aggregate \texttt{info\_attribution}
is reported. A high $E_{\mathrm{CF}}$ with collapsed dual correctness and
non-dominated controls is classified as broken transfer, \emph{not} as a
faithful-but-distorted response: the orthogonal \texttt{transfer\_broken}
annotation captures this.

\subsection{Primary endpoint, statistical discipline, and fair alignment}
\label{sec:stats}

The planned method endpoint is the paired $E_{\mathrm{CF}}$ difference on the
sealed test between a new method and the strongest behavioral baseline
(\texttt{primary\_baseline=kd\_only}), with a template-family-cluster paired
bootstrap (10{,}000 resamples, 95\% CI) and a pair-level sensitivity CI. A gain
would require the CI upper bound $<0$ with no joint-metric degradation;
$\geq3$ seeds and Holm correction are required. This endpoint is unevaluated:
no method was trained and the sealed test was not run. A non-significant
difference is not treated as equivalence; equivalence is assessed only by the
pre-declared two one-sided tests reported in Section~\ref{sec:controltest}. The
archived machine verdict \texttt{distortion\_exists} and the orthogonal
\texttt{transfer\_broken} annotation answer different questions
(Section~\ref{sec:kdbase}). Every table reporting $E_{\mathrm{CF}}$ below gives
the \texttt{template\_family} cluster-paired CI as the primary CI
($n_{\mathrm{clusters}}=8$, \texttt{exploratory=true}) and the pair-level CI as
an explicitly labelled sensitivity.

The arms are aligned on the counterfactual pairs, dtype/kernel, and forward
budget across \texttt{affine}, \texttt{kd\_only}, \texttt{score}, and
mismatched-document controls. Trainable parameter count, training steps and
tuning budget are unequal: the affine map has zero gradient-optimization epochs
(closed-form ridge), whereas the \texttt{kd\_only} baseline has $7{,}340{,}032$
gradient-optimised parameters trained for $8$ epochs. All reported results use
the equal-depth Qwen3-1.7B$\to$Qwen3-0.6B pair (MVP, $28\to28$ identity layer
map); the non-equal-depth Qwen3-4B$\to$Qwen3-1.7B pair appears only in
superseded pre-rebuild artifacts. Runs use CUDA, \texttt{bfloat16} and
\texttt{sdpa}.

rebuild reside mainly in probe/test and cannot be inferred from this validation
contrast. This boundary governs every causal statement in the paper: we report
a controlled version-sensitivity observation, not a single-defect cause. Full
cell-by-cell CIs, the predeclared contrasts, the three prespecified sensitivity
slices, and the non-paired resampling note for cross-version validation
comparisons are deferred to supplementary material; no additional numbers are
introduced here.

\subsection{Adverse results and boundaries}
\label{sec:adverse}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Quality collapse dominates.} On the rebuilt data Joint
      \texttt{dual\_correct} falls to $0.283$--$0.417$ from Self $0.783$--$0.900$,
      while its $E_{\mathrm{CF}}$ is no larger than the mismatched and zero-cache
      controls, close to the randomized control and significantly smaller than
      zero-cache in one seed (Section~\ref{sec:controltest}).
      The broken-transfer label rests on the dual collapse, not on reaching the
      controls.
\item \textbf{Single-signal trigger and mixed controls.} \texttt{transfer\_broken}
      is set by one signal (the Joint-arm dual drop); the seed-0 control
      comparisons are mixed (negative for mismatch and zero, near zero for
      random). The label is a boundary observation.
\item \textbf{Exploratory strength boundary.} All intervals use eight
      \texttt{template\_family} clusters ($<24$), one synthetic equal-depth pair
      and the validation split only; the sealed test was not evaluated.
\item \textbf{Prior positive signals did not survive reconstruction.} The
      version contrast reverses the earlier gap; the corpus changes were applied
      together and no single defect is attributed as the cause. The archived
      mechanism evidence was withdrawn, and no clean-data mechanism experiment
      was run.
\item \textbf{Unverified external validity.} Synthetic-only data and
      \texttt{real=0} bound external validity; the floor-limited SQuAD anchor is
      not used as evidence and no real-document or generated-data result is
      reported.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the study establishes.} The audit shows what can change when a
cache-transfer benchmark is rebuilt. Calibration affects the map itself;
validation changes the tasks and the native reference against which it is
judged. A positive $L$ change can result from a weaker transferred answer, a
stronger native answer, or both. Across the 2$\times$2 design, replacing either
corpus moves $L$ from near zero to $23.3$--$61.7$ points; point estimates keep
their sign across seeds, while cross-version intervals cross zero in several
seeds and all are exploratory (eight families). A second score definition
distinguishes a pattern tied to a single-token approximation from one whose
direction holds under two ways of ranking the same candidates: the
rebuilt-corpus correctness loss retains its direction with an appreciable
change in magnitude (seed~1: $61.7\to40.0$ points on all 60 pairs).

\paragraph{Controls and metric validity.} Joint's counterfactual error is no
larger than the no-information controls and significantly smaller in seed~0,
yet its dual correctness collapses; the pre-declared equivalence test succeeds
in only one of three seeds. Counterfactual error therefore cannot stand in for
transfer utility. Its scale is not explained by the Self denominator alone
(ratio $\approx0.32\to0.80$--$0.90$), its rank correlation with correctness
reverses sign between mappers ($-0.436$ versus $+0.435$), and its intervals do
not separate mappers whose correctness differs by tens of points. Reporting
native and transferred correctness together, with explicit controls, is more
informative than a single discrepancy statistic.

\paragraph{Unresolved evaluations.} The rebuilt corpus changes several
properties at once: single-defect interventions (de-duplication only,
degenerate-pair removal only, entity separation only, template separation only)
have not been run, so no specific flaw is credited for the reversal. The
diagonal scoring check covers only the original/original and rebuilt/rebuilt
cells; the sealed test, a second model pair such as 4B$\to$1.7B, and a
parameter-matched external baseline remain unevaluated. No clean-data mechanism
inference is made.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{One pair, synthetic domain.} All rebuilt-corpus results use one
      equal-depth pair (1.7B$\to$0.6B) on synthetic Chinese templates; the
      non-equal-depth pair appears only in superseded pre-rebuild artifacts.
\item \textbf{Real documents unevaluated.} No real split, manifest entry or run
      exists (\texttt{real=0}); real-document external validity is unverified.
\item \textbf{Exploratory statistics.} Intervals use eight
      \texttt{template\_family} clusters in validation (six in the semantic
      scoring subset), all below 24; the sealed test was not evaluated, so no
      confirmatory endpoint exists. Failed equivalence tests are inconclusive,
      not evidence of a deficit.
\item \textbf{Asymmetric training budget.} The affine map has zero
      gradient-optimization epochs (closed-form ridge, roughly $7.4$M estimated
      coefficients), whereas the \texttt{kd\_only} baseline has $7{,}340{,}032$
      gradient-optimised parameters and $8$ training epochs. Calibration pairs,
      dtype/kernel and forward budget are matched, but trainable parameter
      count, training steps and tuning budget are not; the comparison cannot
      isolate a method effect.
\item \textbf{Single-signal failure label.} \texttt{transfer\_broken} is set by
      the Joint-arm dual drop alone; the seed-0 control comparisons are mixed.
      The label is a boundary observation.
\item \textbf{$E_{\mathrm{CF}}$ is not quality.} A large $E_{\mathrm{CF}}$ can
      coexist with collapsed answer correctness; it is always reported jointly
      with dual success, direction consistency, coverage and control-group
      gaps, and is never compared across scoring readings.
\item \textbf{Limited anchor.} The SQuAD~v2.0 dev anchor is floor-limited for
      the native reader, covers 240 questions from two articles with a single
      reference and seed, and is not used as evidence here.
\item \textbf{Not evaluated.} Single-defect interventions (de-duplication only,
      degenerate-pair removal only, entity separation only, template separation
      only), the sealed test, a second model pair such as 4B$\to$1.7B, and a
      parameter-matched external baseline were not run; no causal attribution
      or cross-model generalization follows from these data.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We set out to measure counterfactual functional fidelity in cross-model KV
handoff. An earlier positive finding was reported on a corpus that an audit
found to contain degenerate must-flip pairs, cross-split document overlap,
overlapping template skeletons and a degenerate bootstrap. Rebuilding the
corpus reverses the finding: the native-minus-transferred dual-correctness gap
moves from $-5.0$--$3.3$ points in the original/original cell to
$23.3$--$61.7$ points across the other nine cell--seed combinations, while the
transferred model's dual correctness collapses to $28.3$--$41.7\%$.

The control and diagnostic results qualify this reversal. Joint's
counterfactual error is no larger than the mismatched and zero-cache controls,
close to the randomized control and significantly smaller than zero-cache in one
seed, yet the pre-declared
equivalence test succeeds in only one of three seeds. Counterfactual error is
not a useful proxy for transfer utility: its scale is not explained by the
Self denominator, its rank correlation with correctness reverses sign between
mappers, and its intervals do not separate mappers whose correctness differs by
tens of points. Full-candidate scoring preserves the direction of the version
contrast in every seed but lowers its magnitude by $21.7$ points in one seed.
We therefore report native and transferred correctness together, with explicit
no-information controls, as the interpretable endpoint.

This is an audit of one synthetic equal-depth model pair with exploratory
intervals (at most eight template families) and no single-defect attribution.
Single-defect interventions, a sealed test, a second model pair and a published
external baseline remain unevaluated; no clean-data mechanism, cross-model
generalization, causal attribution or method improvement is claimed. We release
the repaired benchmark, the numeric ledger and the CPU audit protocol.
